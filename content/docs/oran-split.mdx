## Three units, two interfaces

O-RAN divides a base station into three units. The **O-CU** runs the upper protocol layers. The **O-DU** runs RLC, MAC and the upper part of the physical layer, such as channel coding and modulation. The **O-RU** runs the lower part of the physical layer and the radio itself.

The O-CU and O-DU are connected by the **F1** interface. The O-DU and O-RU are connected by the **Open Fronthaul**, an Ethernet link that carries frequency-domain I/Q samples. Split 7.2x is the point where the physical layer is cut in two.

## What the radio unit does

In split 7.2x the O-RU takes frequency-domain samples from the DU and turns them into a radio signal, and does the reverse for the uplink. Its typical work includes:

- decompressing and compressing I/Q data, often with block floating point (BFP);
- the inverse and forward FFT, and adding or removing the cyclic prefix;
- extracting the random-access (PRACH) signal for the DU;
- doing all of it on a strict slot clock, so that every symbol is ready before its deadline.

## The four fronthaul planes

| Plane | Carries |
| --- | --- |
| **C-plane** | Control messages: which resource blocks and symbols the next U-plane data covers |
| **U-plane** | The I/Q samples themselves, usually compressed |
| **S-plane** | Synchronisation, so that DU and RU share time and frequency |
| **M-plane** | Management: configuration, status and fault reporting |

The C-, U- and S-planes are often grouped together as the CUS-plane. The [BFP codec](/NeoRUwebsite/docs/architecture/virtual-radio/) has been checked against the O-RAN CUS-plane specification.

## Where NeoRU fits

NeoRU is the O-RU in this picture, implemented in software on a commodity server. It is built to connect to existing open CU and DU software rather than to replace it.

<Callout type="warn" title="Interfaces still in progress">
  Fronthaul packet I/O is planned for Phase 2, and validation against a real DU is a later step. The exact interfaces NeoRU implements are defined in its interface specifications, which are pending public release. No interoperability claim is made yet.
</Callout>
